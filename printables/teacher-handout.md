# For my child's teacher — supporting a student who stutters

*One page. Please read before term starts if you can.*

---

**My child stutters.** It is neurological, not psychological. It is not caused by anxiety, tiredness, or anything at home. They are not nervous, and they do not need to calm down.

Stuttering varies day to day and hour to hour. A good day followed by a bad day is normal and does not mean anything went wrong.

---

## The four things that help most

**1. Wait.** Do not finish their words or sentences. The pause feels longer to you than it does to them. Keep normal eye contact and let them land it.

**2. Do not coach.** Please avoid "slow down", "relax", "take a breath", "think about what you want to say", "start again". These are well meant and they make it worse — they tell the child their job is to make speaking comfortable for the listener.

**3. Model it, don't instruct it.** Speaking a little slower yourself, with more pauses, helps far more than any instruction you could give.

**4. Respond to the message, not the delivery.** Show you heard *what* they said.

---

## In the classroom

**Answering questions** — call on them **early** in a round rather than late; waiting builds anxiety. Start with questions that need few words. Telling the whole class that there is plenty of time and quick answers aren't needed helps them without singling them out.

**Reading aloud** — most children who stutter are fluent when reading **in unison** with someone else. Paired or choral reading is the single best accommodation for oral reading. Building from there toward solo reading works well. Letting them see the passage in advance also helps.

**Oral presentations** — please talk to us about options rather than either forcing it or excusing them. Presenting to you alone, to a small group, with a partner, or pre-recorded on video all count as participation. Full exemption is not what we want — it teaches avoidance.

**Marking** — please don't mark down for dysfluency in spoken assessment.

**Academic expectations** — exactly the same as every other student, please.

---

## Teasing

Children who stutter are bullied at far higher rates than their peers, and the classroom is where it usually happens.

- If you see or hear teasing, please address it privately with the other child. Not a debate — teasing is not acceptable.
- Please let us know. We would rather hear about something small than find out about something big later.
- A quiet, private word with our child confirming that stuttering is completely okay with you carries a great deal of weight.

---

## One thing that would help enormously

A brief private conversation with our child in the first week — just asking how they'd like things handled, whether they want to be called on early, and what would make oral tasks easier. Being asked, rather than managed, changes how the whole year goes.
